Salary vs equity is a compensation decision. Both have trade-offs in risk, upside, and long-term value. This guide compares them across every dimension that matters.

Quick Verdict

Salary if you want stability and predictability. Equity if you believe in the company and can handle risk.

Deep Dive: Salary

Salary is fixed cash compensation paid regularly.

Pros of Salary

  • Predictable: Know your income.
  • Liquid: Cash in hand.
  • Simple: Easy to budget.
  • Low risk: No market exposure.

Cons of Salary

  • Capped: Limited upside.
  • No ownership: No stake in company growth.
  • Taxed: Fully taxable.

Who Salary Is For

People who value stability, have financial obligations, or prefer simplicity.

Deep Dive: Equity

Equity is ownership in a company — stocks, options, or shares.

Pros of Equity

  • Upside: Unlimited potential.
  • Ownership: Stake in company success.
  • Tax advantages: Potential capital gains treatment.
  • Alignment: Incentive to grow the company.

Cons of Equity

  • Risk: Can become worthless.
  • Illiquid: Hard to sell before IPO or acquisition.
  • Complex: Tax and vesting rules.
  • Delayed: Value realized later.

Who Equity Is For

Risk-takers, believers in the company, and those with financial cushion.

Head-to-Head

Stability

Salary wins — predictable income.

Upside

Equity wins — unlimited potential.

Risk

Salary wins — low risk.

Long-term value

Equity can win — if the company succeeds.

When to Choose Salary

  • You need stability
  • You have financial obligations
  • You prefer simplicity
  • You're risk-averse

When to Choose Equity

  • You believe in the company
  • You can handle risk
  • You have financial cushion
  • You want ownership

Conclusion

Salary offers stability and predictability. Equity offers upside and ownership but carries risk. Choose based on your risk tolerance, financial situation, and belief in the company.
